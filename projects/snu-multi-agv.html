At Seoul National University's Interactive & Networked Robotics Laboratory, I worked on a warehouse transport concept in which four small AGVs lift and carry one shared platform. My work focused on docking hardware, platform mechanics, rapid prototyping, and ROS 2-based robot development.

Multi-AGV transport architecture

I evaluated how four independently mobile robots could support a single payload platform while remaining easy to dock and deploy. Rather than lowering the entire platform onto the robots, the concept used lift-equipped robot-side connectors to approach and raise the platform from underneath.

Self-aligning docking connector

I iterated several connector concepts to tolerate positioning error during docking, including conical passive alignment, mechanical locking, spring-centering, and a simplified friction-based design. The later concepts were 3D printed to evaluate assembly and fit, revealing the tolerance sensitivity of the more complex mechanism.

A simpler friction-based connector reduced mechanism complexity and was easier to fabricate while still allowing positional tolerance at the interface.

Shared-payload platform

I designed a braced platform structure to distribute load across four support points and reduce twisting during coordinated motion. The frame also preserved open regions required by the robot sensing and localization setup.
