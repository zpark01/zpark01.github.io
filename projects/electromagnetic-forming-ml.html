Developed a Python/PyTorch workflow for uncertainty-aware prediction of electromagnetic forming outcomes at Technische Universität Dresden, working with Fraunhofer IWU researchers to connect experimental manufacturing data, deep learning, and targeted physical testing.

Manufacturing process and experimental data

Electromagnetic forming uses a high-current pulse to generate Lorentz forces that rapidly deform conductive sheet metal. The experimental dataset varied material yield strength, coil-to-workpiece distance, capacitance, and charging voltage, while recording transient current and final forming depth.

Temporal Fusion Transformer process model

I built the preprocessing, training, validation, and inference pipeline for a Temporal Fusion Transformer that combined static process variables with time-dependent measurements to predict forming depth. Quantile regression provided lower, median, and upper predictions rather than only a single point estimate.

Parameter-specific uncertainty and experiment selection

I implemented Monte Carlo dropout to isolate uncertainty associated with individual process inputs rather than treating predictive uncertainty as a single model-wide quantity. This made the model useful not only for prediction, but also for identifying where additional manufacturing data would be most informative.

Starting from a 210-sample experimental dataset, the uncertainty analysis identified three high-uncertainty process combinations and guided 29 additional physical forming tests. Targeted data collection reduced predictive coefficient of variation by as much as 66.7% in the selected region of the parameter space.

Research collaboration

The project combined mechanical-process understanding, experimental manufacturing data, simulation, and machine learning. I worked closely with researchers at TU Dresden and Fraunhofer IWU to translate model uncertainty into practical decisions about where expensive physical experiments were most valuable.
