Led a five-person Cooper Union team to 2nd place in the 2025 Pfizer Digital Hackathon, developing Poko, a digital companion designed to support pediatric cancer patients and their caregivers throughout treatment.

Digital health concept

Pediatric cancer care involves more than treatment itself. Patients and caregivers must manage appointments, medications, symptoms, emotional stress, education, and communication with care teams. We designed Poko as a shared digital ecosystem with separate patient and caregiver experiences.

The patient experience centers on a personalized virtual companion, daily check-ins, missions, learning activities, rewards, and age-appropriate engagement. The caregiver side provides health tracking, scheduling, educational resources, and care-team support.

Caregiver web MVP and AI assistant

We built a functional caregiver web application using React and TypeScript. The MVP included patient monitoring, scheduling, learning resources, daily journals, care-team communication, and progress tracking.

The platform also incorporated an OpenAI API chatbot through LangChain. Rather than allowing unrestricted generation, the assistant was designed to use trusted medical references and avoid emotional or therapeutic responses outside its intended role.

System architecture and safety

Beyond the front-end prototype, we designed a scalable system architecture around a Node.js / Express backend, REST APIs, separate patient and content databases using PostgreSQL and MongoDB, and cloud deployment.

The AI pipeline included middleware for prompt sanitization, response filtering, and interaction logging. The broader product design also considered authentication, role-based access, encryption, HIPAA/COPPA requirements, and separation between patient-facing and caregiver-facing information.

These were treated as system and product requirements rather than simply adding an LLM to the interface, particularly because the intended users include pediatric patients and caregivers in a healthcare setting.

Competition and leadership

As team lead, I coordinated a five-person mechanical and electrical engineering team across user research, product strategy, software development, technical architecture, regulatory considerations, and the final pitch.

We advanced through the virtual round to the final in-person demo and presented the project at Pfizer's New York headquarters to Pfizer Digital professionals. The project received 2nd Place in the 2025 Pfizer Digital Hackathon.
