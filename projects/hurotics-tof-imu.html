A bilateral thigh-mounted sensing system that combines Time of Flight range sensors and IMUs for terrain recognition, gait analysis, and automatic annotation of wearable robot data.

Sensing concept

HUROTICS had large amounts of IMU gait data, but IMUs only measure motion after it occurs and much of the data lacked activity labels. I wanted to test whether low-cost range sensors could add terrain information while also helping label gait data automatically.

I mounted one ToF sensor and one IMU on each thigh. The ToF sensors were tilted about 10 degrees forward so their beams intersected the terrain ahead of the wearer. This made stairs and slopes more visible in the distance signal before direct foot contact.

Embedded system development

The system evolved from an ESP32 prototype into a standalone Teensy 4.1 logger with two VL53L4CD ToF sensors, two BNO085 IMUs, SD storage, activity buttons, and status indication.

During development I resolved duplicate I2C addresses, unreliable BNO085 communication, and IMU packet loss caused by blocking ToF reads. The final architecture used UART-RVC for the IMUs and kept the newest available IMU measurement synchronized with each ToF sample.

Physical validation

Forward tilting produced a much stronger terrain signal. During stair descent, measured distance increased by about 221 mm with the tilted mount compared with about 75 mm using a vertical mount.

Recognition and automatic annotation

With leakage-free time-block validation, combined ToF and IMU features reached 99.0% stair classification accuracy. To test whether manual labels were necessary, I then removed the activity labels and clustered the sensor features directly.

The result suggested that the system could serve not only as a terrain sensor, but also as a low-cost annotation tool for large unlabeled IMU datasets used to train future wearable control models.

Limitations

Stair activities separated strongly, but ramps were harder. Unsupervised agreement dropped to about 68%, and outdoor sunlight substantially increased ToF measurement uncertainty. The dataset also contained one participant across seven sessions, so the results remain a proof of concept rather than cross-subject validation.
