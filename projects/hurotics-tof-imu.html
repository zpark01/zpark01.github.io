A bilateral thigh-mounted sensing system that combines Time of Flight range sensors and IMUs for terrain recognition, gait analysis, and automatic annotation of wearable robot data.

Motivation and sensing concept

HUROTICS already collected large amounts of IMU gait data, but IMUs only measure motion after it occurs and much of the data lacked activity labels. I wanted to test whether inexpensive wearable range sensors could add terrain information and also help label existing gait datasets automatically.

I mounted one ToF sensor and one IMU on each thigh. The ToF sensors were tilted about 10 degrees forward so their beams intersected the ground ahead of the wearer. This geometry makes stairs and slopes visible in the range signal before direct foot contact.

Embedded system development

The logger evolved from an ESP32 prototype into a standalone Teensy 4.1 system with two VL53L4CD ToF sensors, two BNO085 IMUs, SD storage, activity buttons, and status indication. During development I resolved duplicate I2C addresses, unreliable BNO085 communication, and serial packet loss caused by blocking ToF reads.

Physical validation

Forward tilting produced a much stronger terrain signal. During stair descent, measured distance increased by about 221 mm with the tilted mount compared with about 75 mm using a vertical mount.

Recognition and automatic annotation

With leakage-free time-block validation, the combined ToF and IMU features reached 99.0% stair classification accuracy. I then removed the activity labels and clustered the sensor features directly.

The result suggested a second use for the system: automatically organizing unlabeled IMU datasets before they are used to train wearable control models.

Limitations

Stair activities separated strongly, but ramps were harder. Unsupervised agreement dropped to about 68%, and outdoor sunlight substantially increased ToF uncertainty. The dataset also used one participant across seven sessions, so the results remain a proof of concept rather than cross-subject validation.
