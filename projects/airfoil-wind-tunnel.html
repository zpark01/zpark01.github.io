Designed and validated a modular airfoil mounting and pressure-measurement system for Cooper Union's subsonic wind tunnel in ME360 Engineering Experimentation. The rig enables rapid airfoil changes, external angle-of-attack adjustment, and manometer-based surface-pressure testing.

Design and manufacturing

The goal was to replace an earlier setup with a reusable teaching rig that was stable, easy to install, and flexible enough to test different airfoil geometries. An early indexing-plate concept simplified angle adjustment, but manufacturability and tunnel integration led to a more modular final design.

The final assembly used a circular polycarbonate interface, a 1/4-inch support shaft, and modular 3D-printed airfoil sections. The wing could be rotated from outside the tunnel and set between approximately -20° and +20° using a digital angle finder, eliminating the need to open the test section between runs.

Wind-tunnel testing and validation

I calibrated tunnel velocity using a pitot-static probe and inclined manometer, then collected surface-pressure measurements across multiple angles of attack using pressure taps and U-tube manometers. The measured pressure distributions were used to estimate lift and coefficient of lift and compare the experimental results with published NACA 2418 data.

The weighted pressure-integration method matched literature lift-coefficient values within about 1% at +5° and about 5% at +10°. The rig remained rigid without measurable angle drift or vibration during testing up to roughly 41 m/s, while the experiments also identified pressure-tap spacing and limited manometer availability as the main sources of measurement error.
