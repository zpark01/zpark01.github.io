Co-developed passive flow-augmentation structures for small horizontal- and vertical-axis wind turbines, using parametric CAD and SolidWorks Flow Simulation to study how constricted geometries could increase local airflow velocity at the turbine. The work was presented at NCUR 2025.

Parametric aerodynamic design

For the horizontal-axis designs, we varied the difference between the outer inlet diameter and the narrow throat section. A second design family for vertical-axis turbines varied the spherical-cap size, overall height, and central waist geometry.

CFD-guided optimization

We compared the concepts in SolidWorks Flow Simulation using the same air boundary conditions and a 40 in/s inlet velocity. The strongest horizontal-axis geometry produced roughly a 70% local velocity increase, while the best vertical-axis configuration produced about an 18% increase at the constricted region.

Because wind power density scales with the cube of velocity, the simulations suggested substantial theoretical power-density gains under idealized conditions. These results were simulation-based; physical prototyping and wind-tunnel validation were identified as the next steps.
