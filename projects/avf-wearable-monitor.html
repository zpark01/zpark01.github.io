Developed a wearable vibration-sensing system that converts the clinically assessed AVF thrill and bruit into quantitative signals for earlier detection of dialysis-access dysfunction, in collaboration with Mount Sinai nephrologists.

Clinical problem and sensing approach

AV fistulas are routinely checked by touch and sound, making surveillance qualitative and intermittent. We designed a compact wearable system to measure blood-flow-induced vibrations directly through the skin and track changes associated with reduced flow or stenosis.

Piezoelectric sensing and analog front end

My technical work focused on the sensing hardware and signal chain. Two flexible piezoelectric elements were arranged orthogonally to capture directional vibration components. Because the sensors produce high-impedance analog signals, I built a custom buffer and differential-amplifier stage before embedded data acquisition.

The analog front end converted the high-impedance piezo output into a stable signal suitable for acquisition while differential sensing helped suppress common-mode interference.

The processing pipeline combined analog conditioning, AC coupling, 60 Hz notch filtering, and frequency-domain analysis to separate vascular vibration content from electrical noise and motion artifacts.

In-vitro vascular testbed and validation

We built a closed-loop benchtop flow system with a silicone AVF phantom, pump-driven circulation, adjustable branch resistance, and compliance elements to create controlled changes in vascular flow before moving toward clinical validation.

Outflow resistance was progressively increased while pump conditions were held constant, allowing us to simulate gradual fistula restriction and measure how the vibration signature changed.

The proof-of-concept experiments showed that progressive restriction changed both waveform structure and spectral content. The results established sensing feasibility and motivated patient-specific baseline trending rather than a single universal threshold.

Outcome

The project received 2nd Place in the NIH NIBIB / VentureWell DEBUT Challenge with a $15,000 award, and the team presented the work at the 2025 BMES Annual Meeting.
