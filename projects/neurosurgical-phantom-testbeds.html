Designed reusable anatomical testbeds for chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) device validation and intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) surgical training, combining CT-based cranial modeling, silicone tissue analogs, modular surgical access, and iterative testing with neurosurgeons, engineers, and medical-device partners.

Patient-specific CSDH device-validation testbed

I developed a reusable CSDH phantom around patient-derived cranial anatomy, reproducing skull geometry to within <1 mm while making the components that wear during testing modular and replaceable.

The cylindrical fixture allowed the head to be positioned at different surgical orientations. A threaded retention system secured the anatomy, while four configurable burr-hole locations could be opened for testing or closed with threaded plugs when not in use.

I cast a replaceable Dragon Skin 10 silicone dura and an upper-hemisphere silicone brain surrogate to reproduce the compliant internal anatomy and maintain pressure against the subdural cavity.

The modular cavity could be filled by syringe with test fluid or clot analogs, then disassembled, washed, and reused for repeated experiments.

I iterated the cranial-access geometry across four design revisions using surgeon and engineering feedback. The final geometry improved endoscopic visualization by approximately 17% and was evaluated with endoscopes and prototype evacuation devices under realistic access constraints.

Reusable ICH surgical-training phantom

I also redesigned an intracerebral hemorrhage training model that had previously relied on multi-stage gelatin casting and freezing. The new system used custom 3D-printed molds to create a washable silicone brain with a repeatable hemorrhage cavity at a prescribed location and geometry.

A matching skull reproduced the operative approach and burr-hole location so neurosurgeons could repeatedly practice endoscopic visualization and suction through the same constrained trajectory.

Compared with the disposable gelatin workflow, the reusable design reduced fabrication time by approximately 75% and material cost by approximately 40%.

Clinical design iteration

Throughout both systems, I worked between neurosurgeons, engineers, and industry collaborators to translate procedural feedback into mechanical design changes, fabricate revised hardware, and validate each iteration through benchtop and endoscopic testing.
