A first-place project at the FOBISIA Creative Coding Challenge 2019. Our team developed Drifter, a Swift-based augmented-reality game where a black hole is anchored to a real wall and launches obstacles into the player's space. I served as team leader and contributed to game design, implementation, and 3D asset development.

AR gameplay

The game places a portal-like black hole onto a detected wall and uses the surrounding physical space as part of the level. Obstacles, enemies, and pickups emerge from the portal and move toward the player.

The player controls a UFO through a combination of touch and phone movement. While the screen is pressed, the UFO follows the player's movement. When released, the UFO remains at its current position in the AR world, allowing the player to physically reposition the phone without immediately moving the character.

  • Multiple obstacle types with different damage values
  • Health and score tracking
  • Healing pickups
  • Selectable difficulty levels
  • Spatially anchored AR interaction

Concept development

We first explored the environment, player character, obstacle types, and overall gameplay through concept sketches. The black-hole setting became the central visual theme, with hazards and pickups designed around a simple arcade-style survival loop.

3D modeling and visual development

Blender was used to develop the UFO character, portal environment, and obstacle assets before integrating them into the mobile AR experience.

Team leadership and outcome

As team leader, I helped define the game concept, divide responsibilities across design and development, and coordinate the team through implementation. The completed project won 1st Place at the FOBISIA Creative Coding Challenge 2019.
