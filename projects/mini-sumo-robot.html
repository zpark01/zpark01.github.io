Designed and built an autonomous differential-drive mini-sumo robot in a three-person mechatronics team, integrating mechanical hardware, embedded electronics, IR sensing, and real-time motor control on an ATmega328P.

Mechanical and embedded architecture

The robot used a compact two-level chassis to separate the battery and control electronics while staying within competition size constraints. Two DC gearmotors provided differential steering, with a rear ball caster and an adjustable front wedge for opponent engagement.

Sensor-based autonomous control

I worked with an ATmega328P-based control system driving the motors through an L298 motor driver. A 12 V battery powered the drivetrain, while a 5 V regulated rail supplied the microcontroller and sensing electronics.

A downward-facing IR sensor was sampled through the ADC and compared against an adjustable potentiometer threshold. When the robot detected the white ring boundary, the firmware reversed and pivoted back toward the arena; otherwise, it continued its forward search behavior.
